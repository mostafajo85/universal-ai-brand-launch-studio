### Intelligent Audio Tag & Voice Engine

For ElevenLabs speech, choose a live available engine that supports expressive audio tags (prefer Eleven v4 if exposed and confirmed suitable), and an appropriate authorized voice matching the character or narrator and target language/dialect. Discover actual voice IDs and required voice types; retain them for continuity. Read the applicable official ElevenLabs documentation if accessible; distinguish supported guidance from experimental tags and do not assert unverified behavior. Let the agent creatively select, invent where supported, and position natural-language tags appropriate to the individual line, emotional beats, acting, tone, rhythm, emphasis, pauses and intent. No fixed tag list or rigid template. For every Arabic voiceover or dialogue generation, include an explicit dialect/accent tag naming the exact approved Arabic variety, alongside context-appropriate emotion and delivery tags. If the available ElevenLabs engine cannot support the required tags or voice, disclose the blocker before spending and request approval of a supported alternative; do not silently remove tags. For other languages, use explicit accent guidance when relevant. Use the approved dialect in the actual spoken text and the appropriate supported language setting when exposed; do not invent parameters. Avoid contradictory, excessive or unsupported tags. Tags are performance instructions, not spoken words; reject audible tags, repeated/missing words, unwanted language shifts or unnatural delivery. Preserve the exact approved spoken wording and distinguish it from tagged generation text.

Show the script, proposed voice and tagged version, target timing and quote before generating. Use an approved sample or first useful line as a pilot when appropriate; do not require a separate paid sample when an approved final recording can serve as the pilot. Present actual audio for acceptance before downstream video generation or final mixing. Measure duration from real audio, including pauses; adapt scene timing or propose a script adjustment if needed, not unnatural time compression. Record the approved audio asset IDs, voice IDs, engine, duration and script version. For multiple speakers, use separate approved speaker lines/tracks or supported multi-voice generation with verified speaker mapping; preserve turn order and timing.

### Arabic Dialogue Workflow — mandatory default for on-screen Arabic speech

For any visible character speaking Arabic (including UGC and 3D animation), generate and approve the exact spoken audio first using the ElevenLabs workflow above; prepare the approved character/product image references, bind them and the actual approved audio clip(s) to MiniMax H3's supported image/audio reference roles, and generate video following the recorded dialogue and natural lip motion. Never rely on a text-only Arabic dialogue prompt or substitute a filename for attached media. Use the real audio duration and model limits to partition dialogue into natural turns or segments; each MiniMax H3 generation must not exceed the verified limit (known ceiling 15 seconds). Ensure visible speaking character, speaker attribution, silence of other characters where appropriate, spatial continuity and shot timing. Check the resulting audio against the approved recording for exact words, speaker, dialect, emotional delivery and timing; reference support alone does not guarantee unchanged audio. If needed, replace the generated dialogue track with the approved original only if timing/lip sync can be preserved and other sound handled safely; otherwise stop and propose an approved targeted fix. Do not accept altered speech or mismatched lips. A different workflow requires explicit approval and a capability-based reason. This rule is triggered by on-screen Arabic dialogue, not by Arabic narration or Arabic interaction language.

### External voiceover and Shared Audio Master

Cinematic commercials require an externally produced ElevenLabs voiceover by default, in the approved spoken language; use Seedance for visuals, then mix narration, music and SFX in post-production. No lip-sync workflow is needed for off-screen narration. Prevent unintended generated speech; plan clean background sound or replace it in the mix. Produce and approve one narration recording per language/version and reuse the exact same recording and master timing in both aspect ratios. Build one shared final audio mix (narration, music and core SFX), aligned to the same story beats in both videos. Adapt visual timing to that mix rather than regenerating the narration or silently changing the audio. If a format-specific sound change is essential, obtain approval and disclose it; preserve the same narration. Keep approved narration and available stems separately. Any user-requested omission of voiceover or additional localized audio is a scope change, not an automatic fallback.
